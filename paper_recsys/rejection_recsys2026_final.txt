SUBMISSION: 196
TITLE: MV-Align: Multi-View Text Alignment with Explicit Feature Crossing for Sequential Recommendation

-------------------------  METAREVIEW  ------------------------
The paper examines how different LLM "views" can enhance sequential recommendations and how these facets need / can be aligned for improved performance.

Unfortunately, the evaluation setup is unfit for sequential recommenders as the datasets used are not sequential and the leave-one-out strategy (without global cut-off time) is prone to information leaking through time. This part needs to be completely reworked for the results to mean anything.

The scope of the experiments is very narrow as the proposed enhancement is only tested with a single backbone (SASRec) and only on Amazon data, which limits the generality of the findings. The baseline selection is also limited, basically there are no independent baselines only different variations of the same model, making the comparison to be an ablation study effectively. This is also inline with the coverage of related work being superficial.

The novelty of the paper is also questionable, since no individual element of the paper is novel (not the parts of the model or the combination methods), so any novelty is combinatorial, not fundamental.

From the practical standpoint, the contribution of individual views is not clear thus the model's overall complexity might not be justified. This needs further examination.

Overall, while the paper's topic is relevant, the use of flawed evaluation setup, narrow scope, superficial baselines, and practical concerns make it unfit for publication at RecSys.

----------- Strong Points -----------
- The method is described in sufficient detail to support reproducibility, plus the code is share.
- The model is explicitly designed with cold-start scenarios in mind.
- The results are clearly discussed, and practical guidelines for practitioners are provided. The paper includes an analysis of model complexity.
----------- Weak Points -----------
Introduction needs improvement
- There is a significant misalignment between the research problem and objectives presented in the abstract and those introduced in the introduction. As a result, both the research problem and the contribution remain unclear and are insufficiently situated within the current body of research on the topic.
- The research questions are rather simplistic and mostly concern the impact of design decisions on evaluation outcomes. As a rule of thumb, research questions should be complex enough to require critical analysis rather than simple yes/no answers. They should also be clearly aligned with the research problem. In addition, the research questions should be presented *after* the research problem and *before* introducing the contributions.

Design choices are insufficiently motivated
- Why was SASRec selected instead of another sequential recommendation model?
- Several failure modes are mentioned, but these are not supported either by prior literature or by dedicated experiments.
- Why is it important to model feature-level interactions between ID embeddings and text embeddings?
- Why were these particular multi-view representations selected?

Related work
- The related work section is rather superficial.
- Moreover, the comparison between prior work and the proposed approach is not clearly articulated, making it difficult to assess the originality and actual contribution of the paper.

Baselines
- Baselines are effectively ablations of the proposed model rather than strong, independent baselines from related work.

----------- Review -----------
This paper presents MV-Align, a framework for sequential recommendation equipped with multi-view text alignment and explicit feature crossing. The paper has several strengths in terms of idea, reproducibility, and rigor. However, there are many issues that may need to be addressed before publication.

The details of the issues are as follows.

W1: The paper lacks baselines that use text features.

- Existing studies include FDSA, S3-Rec, and UniSRec. The paper should compare the proposed method with such studies (or more recent ones).

W2: Only one backbone model was used in the experiments.

- While this paper proposes a framework for sequential recommendation, only SASRec was used as its backbone model in the experiments. The generalizability of the proposed method to other sequential recommenders remains unclear.

W3: Only one domain was considered in the experiments.

- Only Amazon datasets were used in the experiments. More datasets (e.g., MovieLens) are necessary to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method for other domains and platforms.

W4: It seems like the proposed method cannot handle new items.

- Although the paper focuses on cold-start scenarios, it is unclear how to prepare ID embeddings for new items that do not appear in the training data.
- In the experiments, target items were grouped by popularity with a minimum threshold of one interaction. This means that no new items were used to investigate the performance of the proposed method in cold-start scenarios.

W5: The details and effectiveness of each view are unclear.

- What prompt was used for each view?
- While the multi- and single-view settings were compared in the ablation study, the effectiveness of individual views remains unclear.

W6: The cross-network component tends to deteriorate the HR metric.

- In light of the experimental results, the paper recommends disabling (or enabling) the cross-network component for candidate generation (or final ranking). However, in the former case, a lighter and faster model would be more preferable.
- It is unclear whether this is a problem specific to the proposed method or a common issue with methods that use similar components.

Other comments:

- Section 3.2: What is $\theta_v$? Is it the same as the output of Equation 6 (the variable names are different, though)?
- Section 3.3: While $\alpha_i$ is briefly explained, its mathematical definition is unclear.
- Table 3: The HR@10 score of the proposed method is highlighted for the new stratum. However, the proposed method did not perform best for this condition.

SCORE: -2 (Reject)


----------------------- REVIEW 3 ---------------------

SUBMISSION: 196
TITLE: MV-Align: Multi-View Text Alignment with Explicit Feature Crossing for Sequential Recommendation

----------- Strong Points -----------
1. Rigorous, transparent, and extensive experiments
f2. The paper contributes some interesting and useful empirical findings, namely that TF-IDF is a surprisingly strong baseline, and that there is an HR-ranking trade-off determined by the cross network which influences deployment choices
3. Code, configuration files, and run scripts are released on a RecBole-based anonymous repository with public datasets, ensuring strong reproducibility
----------- Weak Points -----------
1. The framework's complexity might not be justified by its marginal benefit, and the authors' own ablations suggest near-redundant components. MV-Align stacks many parts on top of SASRec (i.e., four LLM prompt views, per-view SVD, ZCA whitening, SE-style gating, l2 normalization, learned view gating, a DCN-V2 cross network, multi-view InfoNCE alignment, separate training-time and inference-time cold-start reweighting, and a two-phase schedule) each with its own hyperparameters. Yet the full pipeline beats the best text baseline (TF-IDF+single-view LLM) by only ~3% HR@10 (Beauty 5.62->5.79; Toys 6.39->6.61), most of the text benefit already comes from cheap TF-IDF. Moreover, the paper's own ablations report that removing SE gating causes "only marginal changes" and that whitening matters only for the multi-view design they chose. The gains are statistically significant (well-tested), but the design complexity and tuning surface are not clearly justified by the marginal benefit.
2. There is a narrow scope and no external baselines evaluated. Evaluation covers two similar, very sparse Amazon domains, one backbone (SASRec), and one LLM (Qwen2.5-7B). The deliberate layered-baseline design means there is no comparison to existing text-enhanced sequential recommenders, so MV-Align's competitiveness with other approaches, and the generality of the findings across domains/backbones/LLMs, are not established.
3. MV-Align assembles established components (e.g., SASRec, DCN-V2 cross network, SENet-style gating, ZCA whitening, InfoNCE alignment, multi-prompt LLM views) into a new recipe. The combination and the DCN-V2 application to ID x Text fusion in sequential recommendation are reasonable, but no individual element is novel

----------- Review -----------
This is an extensive empirical study. The evaluation protocol is a clear strength: full-ranking metrics (rather than sampled), four seeds with reported variance, paired significance tests against the strongest baseline on large test sets, popularity-stratified analysis, ablations cleanly mapped to your research questions, hyperparameter-sensitivity curves, and a FLOPs/parameter accounting. The layered baseline design is the right way to isolate the marginal contribution of each text component under a shared backbone, and the practical framing (i.e., TF-IDF is a strong baseline, the cross network induces an HR-ranking trade-off mapping to candidate-generation vs. re-ranking, and multi-view modestly beats single-view at equal bandwidth) offers actionable guidance. Moreover, the work appears to be highly reproducible.

My main concerns are about the size of the contribution relative to its complexity. The full MV-Align pipeline is elaborate (i.e., four LLM views plus whitening, SE gating, l2 normalization, view gating, a DCN-V2 cross, InfoNCE alignment, two separate cold-start mechanisms, and a two-phase schedule), yet its improvement over the best text baseline (TF-IDF+single-view LLM) is small (~+0.17-0.22 pp HR@10, ~3%). The results show TF-IDF already captures most of the text benefit, and the ablations indicate that some components (notably SE gating) contribute little. The gains are statistically significant, but it would strengthen the paper considerably to demonstrate which components are actually necessary, e.g., whether a substantially simpler variant (say, TF-IDF+LLM with whitening and alignment only) matches MV-Align, and to make the cost-benefit case for the added complexity explicit.

Second, the evaluation is confined to two similar, very sparse Amazon domains, one backbone, and one LLM, and  omits any external text-enhanced sequential recommender; the layered design is well-justified for the RQs, but without at least one external baseline (e.g., a UniSRec-style method) and a second domain or backbone, it is hard to judge MV-Align's competitiveness or the generality of the findings.

Third, the cold-start story is mixed: on Beauty, adding text (including MV-Align) yields HR_new below ID-only (1.76 vs. 1.88), so the cold-start benefit rests on NDCG/MRR rather than HR. I'd state clearly that the cold-start benefit shows up in NDCG/MRR but not Hit Rate, and explain why text lowers new-item Hit Rate on Beauty.

----------- Key Clarification -----------
1. Given that TF-IDF captures most of the gain, SE gating is "marginal," and MV-Align adds only ~3% HR@10 over TF-IDF+LLM, can a substantially simpler variant (e.g., TF-IDF+LLM with whitening + alignment only -- dropping SE gating, dual cold-start reweighting, and the two-phase schedule) match MV-Align? Which components are actually necessary for the reported gains?
2. Do the findings (whitening helps multi-view; cross HR-ranking trade-off; multi-view > single-view) hold on a second domain/backbone or with a different LLM, or are they specific to sparse Amazon data + SASRec + Qwen2.5-7B?
3. How does MV-Align compare to at least one existing text-enhanced sequential recommender (e.g., UniSRec-style)? Without this, competitiveness with the field  is unclear.
4. Why does adding text reduce new-item HR below ID-only? Is the cold-start benefit best characterized as ranking-quality (NDCG/MRR) rather than HR?
